    Answers, not AI jargon

    Questions people actually ask about SEO and AI search

    These are written to be useful to a business owner who wants to know what all this "GEO" talk actually means.

    Google AI searchUpdated 29 September 2026

    Do I need special SEO for Google AI Overviews?

    No special Google "GEO" markup is required. Google says the same foundational SEO practices used for normal Search apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. A page still needs to be crawlable and eligible for Search, and its important information should be available as text. Google also says there is no special schema.org markup required specifically for AI features.

    That does not make the page irrelevant to AI search. It makes the fundamentals more important: clear information, useful content, internal links, good page experience and accurate business information.

    Source: Google Search Central — AI features and your website.

    AI crawler accessOpenAI / Anthropic / Perplexity

    Can ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity see my website?

    They can use different crawlers and user agents. OpenAI publishes OAI-SearchBot for search, Anthropic publishes Claude-SearchBot for search and Claude-User for user-directed retrieval, and Perplexity publishes PerplexityBot for search and Perplexity-User for user actions.

    For a search citation, the important practical question is whether the relevant search crawler can reach the public content. Robots.txt is one of the controls these services document.

    Sources: OpenAI crawler documentation, Anthropic crawler guidance and Perplexity crawler documentation.

    Structured dataWhat schema can and cannot do

    Does adding Schema make ChatGPT cite my business?

    No. Structured data can make a page's entities and relationships easier for search systems to understand, and Google supports it for eligible Search features. But Google's current AI-search guidance explicitly says there is no special schema required for AI Overviews or AI Mode.

    Think of JSON-LD as a description layer, not a citation switch. The visible page still needs to be clear, useful and crawlable, and the structured data needs to match what people can actually see.

    Sources: Google: AI features and your website and Google: Introduction to structured data.

    Local searchUK + international research

    Does local SEO still matter now that people use AI?

    Yes, but local search is becoming more multi-channel. Google says complete and accurate Business Profile information makes a business more likely to appear in relevant local results. Separate 2026 BrightLocal research found that 31% of US consumers use AI tools for local-business recommendations at least monthly, while 73% said their most recent local-business search started on a mobile device.

    For a trade business, that means the basics should agree everywhere: who you are, what you do, where you work, how to contact you and what customers say about you.

    Sources: Google Business Profile Help and BrightLocal — Consumer search behavior 2026.

    ReviewsBrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026

    Do reviews affect whether people choose a trade business?

    They are a major part of the research process. BrightLocal's 2026 US consumer survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses. It also found that 54% visit a business's website after reading positive reviews.

    That is useful for GEO because an AI recommendation is not the end of the journey. People may still verify the business, inspect its website and compare evidence before making contact. Reviews, the website and business listings therefore need to tell the same story.

    Source: BrightLocal — Local Consumer Review Survey 2026. Survey population: 1,002 US adults.

    AI summariesPew Research Center, US

    Do AI search citations actually send people to websites?

    Not necessarily, and the numbers are a useful warning against promising traffic from citations alone. Pew Research Center analysed 68,879 Google searches in March 2025 from browsing data contributed by 900 US adults. On searches with an AI-generated summary, users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, versus 15% when there was no AI summary, and clicked a link in the AI summary itself in just 1% of visits.

    A citation can still expose a business to a user who wants to verify the answer. The sensible objective is therefore broader than "get a citation": make the site good enough to be found, understood, trusted and contacted.

    Source: Pew Research Center. Population: US adults who shared browsing data; fieldwork March 2025.

    What the score means

    A transparent checklist, not an "AI authority" score

    There is no Google-issued GEO score and no credible number that guarantees a ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity citation. The checker therefore scores only what it can inspect directly.

    What it can check: HTML, headers, canonical, robots directives, structured-data presence, visible text, headings, links, contact signals and common crawler rules.

    What it cannot prove: rankings, Search Console data, Google Business Profile completeness, review authenticity, Core Web Vitals from real users, or whether an AI system will cite you for a particular query.
